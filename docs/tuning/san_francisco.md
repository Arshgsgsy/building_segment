# San Francisco over-segmenter knobs (sheet sanborn00813_008_0005, 3 November 2026)

I set `san francisco: {ink_contrast: 15, ink_close: 5, ink_L_dark: 35}` in `oversegment_by_city` and changed nothing else (shared knobs, `labels`/`fit`, code, label folders and Denver untouched). There are no labels for this sheet, so I judged on label-free checks inside four hand-drawn block outlines (`img/blocks.jpg`, harness `sf.py`) and on crops. First I found that the two alarming numbers were not the real problem. The 47% fill coverage is a metric artifact: the yellowed paper border and the stain have chroma above 10, so they count as "fill" (stain C* 25, border about 9-10, against pink 21, green 28, yellow 72). Inside the blocks the defaults already cover 97.7% of the colored fill, and fill_chroma 13 or 16 only made the CLI number look better without finding any building. The 47 "dark" flags are all bits of the binding cloth at x < 110 (`img/t7_binding.png`), off the map. The real failure was uncut walls. On the dark paper the defaults miss faint walls, wall ends that stop short of the next wall, the 15-20 px wide gray-blue outlines of iron-clad sections (L* about 50, too wide and too low-contrast for the black-hat at 20), and several pink/green seams. I measured this as straight wall-network ink (shared-knob ink mask) with the same region on both sides, plus "mixed" regions holding two fill colors. With the defaults that is 15,199 uncut wall px in 50 wall lines of 30 px or more, and 15 mixed regions (358k px of a second color).

`ink_contrast` 15 and `ink_close` 5 bring uncut wall px to 3,395 (-78%) and the wall lines to 9. When I looked at those 9 (`img/uncut_walls_base_vs_tuned.png`), none was a real solid wall between buildings. They are box symbols, letter strokes, dimension crosses and partition stubs that end inside a room. Mixed regions go 15 → 12 (358k → 195k px). The changes that matter most are the iron-clad box at Commercial St, now its own region apart from the brick buildings above and below (`img/t5_ironclad.png`), the Office and Shed by the stain cut apart (`img/t6_stain.png`), the brick "Com'sn Store" split from the green Printing building (`img/t1_rows_nw.png`), and the green Lime building partly split from its pink neighbours on the held-out crop (`img/h5_se_lodgings.png`). `ink_L_dark` 35 turns the binding cloth (L* 25-33) into ink: dark flags go 47 → 2 and regions outside the blocks go 72 → 31. Inside the blocks it only removed two slivers between a boiler symbol and its outline. Regions go 519 → 853 (822 in the blocks), and suspects go 31 → 27. The diff reports 6 "merges" against the defaults. I looked at every one, and each is the loss of a false bridge cut through skylight boxes or text, not a wall (`img/apparent_merges_are_lost_false_cuts.png`). Small isolated fill objects found go 10/15 → 9/15. The one lost is the gray iron-clad band itself, and none of the misses in either run is a shed. Regions in the stain go 8 → 6, and they are compass spokes and the outlined letters of "Volume Two", not buildings. I tuned on eight crops (`img/t*.png`) and looked at the six held-out crops (`img/h*.png`) only after choosing the values. They show the same picture, with no wall that the defaults cut and the tuned run leaves uncut.

The cost is holes and fragments. Lower contrast plus a wider closing turns large lettering, stair hatching and the iron-clad bands into ink, so block fill coverage drops from 97.7% to 96.4% (holes of 150 px or more go 289 → 444). Text-heavy buildings such as C.E. Whitney and the Great Am. Importing Tea Co. come in many pieces with ragged gaps between the letters (`img/h6_sw_market.png`). I checked whether this survives labeling by grouping the tuned regions by the default region they mostly lie in and running the real `dissolve` with the shared wall fit (`dissolve_check.py`). Building area in the blocks goes from 9.28M to 9.21M px (-0.75%). What is still missing is mostly the gray iron-clad bands between two buildings, bigger holes at hatched stairs and a few text notches along walls (`img/dissolve_losses.png`). I chose 15/5 over 17/5 (4,318 uncut px, smaller holes) because only 15 cuts the pink/green seams. Going further gains little: 13/5, bridge_gap 80 and ink_width 21 cut a few more stubs, mostly text, and make more holes. I left bridge_length, min_area and fill_chroma shared. Lowering bridge_length cut mostly through text, min_area 300 added junk for no new structure, and fill_chroma 13 only removed two stain regions while eroding pale strips. Two problems are still left for hand cuts, and both need code, not knobs. Adjacent buildings of different colors with only a dashed line or no line between them stay one region: yellow Bolting Rm with green Forges, yellow Rest't with green Sal & Lunch, the green 50' section on top of Whol. Paints, and Ship Chandler (`img/remaining_color_merges.png`). Rows split only by dashed lines also stay whole, such as the East St. stores at 1-7 and 9-17 (`img/t8_east_st.png`), though these may be partitions within one building. I also lost the "round" flag on the fire-alarm circle, which now comes as four pieces. Everything else is in this folder: `results.jsonl` (all 53 runs, from `sweep1.sh` to `sweep3.sh`), `table.py`, `diff.py`, `lost.py`, `small.py`, `mixed.py`, `montage.py`, `boxes.py`, `show.py` and `runs/` (label maps). ## Hue cut and dashed-line cut (3 November 2026, later)

The color merges above needed code, so I added an optional step 4d to the over-segmenter with three new knobs in the shared `oversegment:` block: `hue_cut` and `dash_cut` both default to 0 (off), and `hue_chroma` is 15. With both off, the code path is exactly the old one, so Aliquippa, Uhrichsville, Galveston and Denver are unchanged. A control run with the San Francisco knobs and the cut off (`e_tuned`) reproduces the earlier numbers exactly. Only the `san francisco:` entry turns them on, with `hue_cut: 20` and `dash_cut: 90`. The hue cut works per region. I average a* and b* over the fill only, leaving out text, walls and dashes, with a 2 px Gaussian, so the fill color carries across letters and dashed lines. Then I run a 2-means on the hue angle of each region's pixels, starting from the 10th and 90th percentiles so stray halo pixels can't claim a center. If the two centers are at least `hue_cut` degrees apart and each holds `min_area` px, every pixel goes to its nearer group and the region is cut along the group boundary, in two passes for three-color regions. I first tried cutting along hue edges instead, but any one-pixel gap where text or a W.N. mark sat on the seam let the two colors stay joined (Lime and Ship Chandler never split that way). A partition boundary has no gaps. The measured fill hues explain the threshold: pink sits near -20° to 0°, yellow near 100° (C* about 70) and green near 125° (C* about 28). Yellow against green is the closest pair at about 25°, and 15, 20 and 25 give the same 26 splits, while 30 leaves 2 mixed regions. So I took 20, the middle of that plateau. `hue_chroma` 12, 15 and 20 also give the same result.

On the whole sheet (`sweep4.sh`, runs `e_*` in `results.jsonl` and `runs/`), regions spanning two fill colors in the blocks go 12 → 0 (195k → 0 px of a second color), regions in the blocks go 822 → 890, and uncut wall px go 3,395 → 3,179. The 9 uncut wall lines are exactly the same 9 as before, so the cut adds no new uncut wall. `diff.py` finds no merges against the tuned run. I looked at all 26 hue splits and every one follows a real color boundary (`img/hue_cut_fixes.png`): yellow Bolting Rm from green Forges, yellow Rest't from green Sal & Lunch, the green 50' section of Whol. Paints, the green Oil Storage box inside the Ship Chandler building, the green Ship Chandler Oil & Tar store, and Lime from the pink warehouse around it. It also splits green boxes inside pink buildings (Printing, Plumb'g & Tin Shop, Hand Print'g, Tin & Plumb'g), yellow roof structures on brick buildings (the condensing coils on C.E. Whitney) and the yellow Candy strip. The cost is small. Block fill coverage goes 96.38% → 96.31%, holes of 150 px or more go 444 → 452, and about 7k px of thin color strips along walls now fall to the sliver filter. Stain regions stay at 6, dark flags go 2 → 1 and suspects go 27 → 22.

The dashed-line cut reuses `sanborn.labels.dash_lines`, the function the dissolve wall fit uses, with `dash_cut` as its minimum line length. On its own it fixes no color merge and adds about 27 regions. With the hue cut it splits the Sal & Lunch store from the 11-17 stores on East St. and cuts dashed partitions such as the S. / Sal. rooms and the Lodgings rows. It finds no merges, and 45 and 90 behave almost the same, so I kept the longer 90. It only detects straight horizontal and vertical dash rows, so the slanted and broken dashed lines of the East St. stores at 1-7 still stay whole, and those may be partitions anyway. Small isolated fill objects found go 9/15 → 8/15 with it, but the one lost is the hatched fill of a stair next to a dashed line on East St., not a building. I did not check gray-blue iron-clad fill on its own. Its chroma is near or below the 15 floor (the harness counts it from 8), so the hue cut likely does little there, and the ink knobs already handle most of those outlines. `tests/test_oversegment.py` now has a test on a real 280 x 220 crop of this sheet (`tests/data/sf_restt_sal_lunch.png`, Rest't and Sal & Lunch), which comes out as one region without the cut and as two with the San Francisco knobs.
